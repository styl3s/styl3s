# Day 02 — Blemish Removal Tool Mastery

**Time budget:** 60 minutes
**Where this fits:** Week 1 — Foundations & Non-Destructive Fluency

## Why today looks like this

The diagnostic showed you already know *when* to reach for each healing
tool (Spot Healing vs. Healing Brush vs. Patch vs. Clone Stamp) and that
edges need more care than open cheek areas — that's correct, conceptual
ground you don't need re-taught. What today builds is the **muscle memory**
and tool-setting fluency so those choices become fast and automatic,
because once frequency separation enters the workflow on Day 04, you'll be
making these same tool decisions on a blurred low-frequency layer where
mistakes are harder to spot. Better to build the reflexes on a normal image
first.

**Note on layering:** today's drills happen on a flattened duplicate of
your Day 01 photo(s), *not* inside the `01–05` template groups. Real
production blemish removal will live inside `02 Frequency Separation`
starting Day 04 — today is purely about getting fast and correct with the
tools themselves before that added complexity.

## Objectives

1. Get fluent with sampling modes and settings on all four healing/cloning
   tools, not just knowing what they're for.
2. Build correct judgment in practice, not just in theory, for edge cases
   (hairline, jawline, ears) vs. open skin.
3. Get a timed baseline for a full blemish pass on one photo.

## Agenda

### 0:00–0:05 — Setup
Open one of your Day 01 photos. Duplicate the layer (flatten if needed) —
this is a scratch layer for tool drills, separate from your template.

### 0:05–0:20 — Spot Healing Brush & Healing Brush
- **Spot Healing Brush:** run it over 4-5 small, isolated blemishes
  (open cheek/forehead areas). Toggle between **Content-Aware**,
  **Create Texture**, and **Proximity Match** in the options bar on the
  same blemish and compare results — notice which mode handles skin
  texture best (usually Proximity Match or Content-Aware, not Create
  Texture, on skin).
- **Healing Brush:** pick 2-3 slightly larger or textured blemishes.
  Practice Alt-clicking to set an explicit source point close to the
  target in tone/texture, then healing. Notice how much more control
  you have over *where the texture comes from* vs. Spot Healing's guess.

### 0:20–0:35 — Patch Tool
Find (or simulate by picking a shadow/shine area) a larger imperfection —
under-eye shadow, a bigger blemish cluster, stray shine on the forehead.
- Draw a selection with the Patch Tool, drag it to a clean source area.
- Try both **Normal** and **Content-Aware** patch modes and compare.
- Note when Patch beats Healing Brush: larger areas, and when you want to
  drag-preview the result before committing.

### 0:35–0:45 — Clone Stamp on edge cases
This is the one that needs the most deliberate practice, per the
diagnostic. Work specifically on:
- A blemish or flyaway **right at the hairline** — small brush, low flow
  (~30-40%), sample from nearby skin with similar light, multiple light
  passes rather than one heavy one.
- A mark **near the jawline** where healing tools tend to smear the edge
  between face and background/neck — same approach: small brush, low
  flow, build up gradually.
- Compare the result to what Spot Healing/Healing Brush would have done
  in the same spot (undo and try) — you should be able to see *why*
  Clone Stamp's manual control avoids the smearing.

### 0:45–0:55 — Timed pass
On a fresh copy of a photo, do a complete basic blemish pass — every
tool as appropriate — and time yourself. This isn't about being fast yet,
it's about establishing a real number.

### 0:55–1:00 — Wrap-up
Log your timed-pass number. Note any tool/setting combination that felt
slow or uncertain — that's what to watch for as frequency separation adds
a layer of complexity starting Day 04.

## What today covered

- Sampling-mode fluency on Spot Healing Brush and Healing Brush, and when
  each beats the other.
- Patch Tool for larger imperfections, Normal vs. Content-Aware.
- Deliberate Clone Stamp practice on hairline/jawline edge cases — the
  exact gap the diagnostic flagged — using small brush size, low flow,
  and multiple light passes instead of one heavy one.
- A timed baseline for a basic blemish pass, to compare against once
  frequency separation is in the mix.

**Carrying forward to Day 03:** you'll build masking skill (feathering,
gradients, refine edge, intro to luminosity masks) on top of today's tool
fluency — masking is what will let you apply these same healing/cloning
edits with more precision and control.
